General

Working with a dietitian

How do I find a good dietitian or dietician in Mumbai?

Look for someone with a clinical qualification rather than a short certification, and ask what they review before writing a plan. A clinical dietitian should want your thyroid, blood sugar, insulin, vitamin and iron reports before recommending anything. Himanshi Daryani holds a master's in specialised dietetics with diabetes and cardiac nutrition, and is a Certified Diabetes Educator and Monash-trained Low FODMAP specialist.

What is the difference between a dietitian and a nutritionist?

In India the two words are used loosely and often interchangeably. In practice, a clinical dietitian is trained to work with diagnosed medical conditions such as diabetes, PCOS, IBS and high cholesterol, and to interpret pathology reports. A nutritionist may work more broadly on general wellness. Himanshi practises as a clinical dietitian and nutritionist across both.

Where do consultations happen?

Everything is online. Himanshi is based in Mumbai and consults over video with clients anywhere in India, as well as with Indian clients living abroad. Reports are reviewed in advance, a full history is taken in the first session, and there is direct messaging support between appointments.

Will I be given a fixed diet chart?

No. A printed chart works for the two weeks you can tolerate it and then stops. The approach here is to identify what is driving the problem, then build eating patterns around the food your household already cooks, adjusting as your reports and your life change.

Can you help with weight loss specifically?

Yes, and it is one of the most common reasons people arrive. The difference is that weight is treated as a symptom first. Thyroid dysfunction, PCOS and insulin resistance are ruled in or out before any deficit is set, because a plan that ignores the driver is the reason weight came back the previous times.

Do you work with vegetarian and Jain diets?

Yes. Most plans built here are vegetarian or eggetarian, and Jain households are catered for regularly. Protein density is the usual gap in Indian vegetarian diets, and it is solvable without changing what your family eats.

Weight loss

Weight loss

How is a clinical dietitian different from a regular diet plan?

A diet plan tells you what to eat. A clinical dietitian works out why your body is responding the way it is first. That means reviewing your thyroid, blood sugar, insulin, vitamin and iron markers before building anything, so the plan addresses the actual driver rather than guessing. It also means the plan is adjusted as your reports and your life change.

Is this done online?

Yes, entirely. There is no clinic visit involved. Sessions run over video, reports are shared digitally beforehand, and you get direct messaging support between appointments. Clients join from across India and from abroad.

How much weight can I expect to lose, and how quickly?

No honest dietitian will give you a number before seeing your reports. Rate of change depends on your starting point, thyroid and insulin status, muscle mass, sleep and medication. The goal here is a rate you can hold once the programme ends, not the fastest possible drop.

I am vegetarian. Will I get enough protein?

Yes. Most Indian vegetarian diets are not short on food, they are short on protein density and spread across the day. Dals, dairy, paneer, soya, eggs where included, and correctly used supplements can comfortably meet requirements. Protein targets are built around what your household already cooks.

Will I have to give up rice, roti or my usual food?

No. Elimination is not the method here. Meal sequencing, pairing and portioning change how the same roti or rice behaves in your body. A plan you cannot eat at a family dinner is a plan that fails in week three.

Can you help if I have PCOS or thyroid issues alongside weight gain?

That is the usual case rather than the exception. PCOS, hypothyroidism and insulin resistance all make weight resistant to standard advice, and each needs a different approach. Identifying which one is leading is the first part of the work.

PCOS

PCOS & hormones

What is the best diet for PCOS?

There is no single best PCOS diet, and any plan sold as one is guessing. Around seventy per cent of women with PCOS have insulin resistance as the leading driver, but for others inflammation, chronic stress or an untreated thyroid issue is doing more of the work. The right plan follows from identifying which one is dominant in your case, which is why reports come before food changes.

Can PCOS be reversed with diet alone?

PCOS is managed rather than cured, but the symptoms that bother people most — irregular cycles, weight that will not shift, acne, hair fall — often improve substantially when the underlying driver is addressed. Many women see cycle regularity return. Nutrition is one part of that alongside your gynaecologist's care.

Why can I not lose weight with PCOS even when I eat very little?

Eating very little is frequently part of the problem. Chronic under-eating combined with heavy cardio raises stress hormones and lowers thyroid output, which makes weight loss harder in a condition already marked by insulin resistance. The usual correction is eating more protein, more consistently, with strength training replacing some of the cardio.

Do I need to go gluten free or dairy free for PCOS?

Usually not. Blanket gluten and dairy elimination is one of the most repeated pieces of PCOS advice online and one of the least supported for most people. Elimination is used here only where there is a specific reason to suspect it, and always with a plan to reintroduce.

Is PCOS work suited to online consultations?

It is particularly well suited to it. PCOS work runs on reports, cycle tracking and regular check-ins rather than physical examination, so nothing is lost by doing it over video. All consultations here are online.

Will this help if I am trying to conceive?

Nutrition support for PCOS commonly runs alongside fertility treatment, and improving insulin sensitivity and cycle regularity is often part of that picture. Any fertility decisions stay entirely with your gynaecologist or fertility specialist.

Diabetes

Diabetes & blood sugar

Can a dietitian help reduce my HbA1c?

Nutrition is one of the strongest levers available for type 2 diabetes and prediabetes, and structured changes to meal composition, sequencing and timing frequently move HbA1c meaningfully. How far it moves depends on how long the condition has been running, your medication and your starting numbers. Any medication changes are decided by your physician, not here.

Do I have to stop eating rice and roti?

No. Blanket carbohydrate elimination is not the method, and it is a common reason people abandon diabetes diets within weeks. What changes is quality, portion, what you eat alongside the carbohydrate and what order you eat it in. The same rice can produce a very different glucose curve.

Is prediabetes worth treating, or should I wait and recheck?

Prediabetes is the stage where effort has the largest return, and the stage most people are told to simply wait through. Insulin resistance can run for years before fasting glucose looks abnormal, which is why fasting insulin — a test most standard panels skip — often tells the story long before HbA1c does.

Do you work with type 1 diabetes as well?

Yes. Himanshi is a certified Type 1 Diabetes Educator through SPEED. The work covers carb counting you can trust, how fat and protein delay a rise for hours afterwards, planning around exercise, and strategies for eating out, travel and festivals. Insulin always stays with your endocrinologist.

Is diabetes work suited to online consultations?

Yes, and it translates especially well. The work runs on reports, glucose logs and CGM data, all of which are shared digitally anyway. All consultations here are online, wherever you are in India.

Will I need to stop my diabetes medication?

That decision belongs entirely to your treating physician. Nutrition work runs alongside your prescription, and your doctor is kept informed as your numbers change so that they can adjust if and when they judge it appropriate.
