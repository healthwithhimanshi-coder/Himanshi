What’s usually driving it

Type 2 diabetes is rarely about one dessert. Insulin resistance builds quietly over years: your cells stop responding well to insulin, and the pancreas works harder to compensate. By the time glucose shows up on a report, the process has been running a long time. Sleep, stress, muscle mass and meal timing all feed into it, which is why food alone is never the whole answer — and why cutting sugar on its own so often fails to move the number.

How we work on it
  • Meal sequencing and pairing, so the same roti spikes you less
  • Carb quality and portioning, instead of blanket carb elimination
  • Protein and fibre floors at every meal, built for an Indian kitchen
  • Movement placed where it does the most for your glucose
  • For type 1: carb counting you actually trust, and fewer hypo surprises
  • Coordination with your physician, who stays in charge of medication
